# AI subsystem authority, validation, and promotion contract

## Summary

The AI subsystem is a replaceable proposal layer inside the verification
engine. Models may atomize claims, diversify queries, rank candidates, locate
passages, compare scope, propose origin links, translate, synthesize a draft,
search for contradictions, and triage monitoring events. They may not admit
evidence, assert source independence, close an open-world search, grant an
assurance status, change a certificate, authorize a tool, or write canonical
state.

Every model call consumes immutable, purpose-limited inputs and emits a
schema-validated proposal. The system retains the observable invocation,
output, validation, acceptance decision, and applicable calibration envelope.
It does not depend on hidden chain-of-thought, model self-confidence, model
consensus, or exact regeneration of a hosted model response. Deterministic
checks and appropriately qualified humans remain the only gates that can
promote a proposal into the dossier; the deterministic assurance kernel remains
the only component that can authorize certificate issue.

This document is an **active research proposal**. It does not describe accepted
Parallax behavior or an implemented subsystem.

## Defines

The proposed authority boundary for AI models, stage-specific permitted and
forbidden roles, structured output and invocation manifests, replay limits,
calibration and abstention rules, provider portability, injection and poisoning
controls, stage evaluations, baselines, promotion and fallback rules, and the
conditions that would falsify or narrow the subsystem.

## Does not define

An approved implementation, a model or search vendor, final JSON Schemas,
production prompts, a universal confidence threshold, domain-specific evidence
methods, reviewer staffing, certificate semantics, or permission to process a
particular source or person's data.

## Research status and governing boundary

- **Status:** active research proposal; no model, stage, threshold, or provider
  is approved.
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-08-12.
- **Epistemic owner:** the [assurance model](assurance-model.md) defines what an
  issued result may mean.
- **Runtime owner:** the [architecture](architecture.md) keeps models outside
  canonical and issuance authority.
- **Workflow owner:** the
  [verification protocol](verification-protocol.md) defines the full pipeline,
  human roles, immutable attempts, and kernel transitions.
- **Security owner:** the [threat model](threat-model.md) defines acquisition,
  model, injection, poisoning, privacy, rights, and incident boundaries.

The subsystem follows one non-compensatory rule:

> A model can reduce search or review work only inside a measured stage. No
> amount of model quality, agreement, fluency, or provider diversity can replace
> a missing source, unresolved scope, failed deterministic predicate, required
> expert judgment, or publication authorization.

## Authority boundary

### What models may do

Models may:

- propose typed candidates against frozen inputs;
- expose alternatives, ambiguity, missing information, and explicit
  abstention;
- attach every material proposal to source, artifact, locator, query, or other
  canonical references;
- generate concise, inspectable reason codes and evidence-bound explanations;
- challenge another proposal or create a deliberately different search route;
- operate in shadow mode and assist a qualified reviewer; and
- be removed, replaced, or bypassed without changing canonical semantics.

### What models may never do

Models may never:

- write or mutate a `Claim`, `ArtifactVersion`, `EvidenceUse`,
  `OriginAssertion`, `Evaluation`, `Certificate`, policy, rights record, or
  audit event directly;
- decide that a source is admissible, lawful, independent, complete, authentic,
  methodologically sound, or true;
- convert retrieval rank, source count, citation presence, signature validity,
  or model agreement into evidentiary weight;
- infer a universal negative from an unsuccessful search;
- raise an outcome ceiling, waive an abstention condition, resolve a material
  reviewer disagreement, or issue, restore, supersede, restrict, or withdraw a
  certificate;
- execute source-supplied instructions, authorize tools, select credentials,
  expand network access, or disclose protected inputs; or
- grade the final system solely against labels or rationales generated by the
  same model family.

AI adapters have no certificate key, policy-write credential, direct canonical
database write path, unrestricted network route, or cross-tenant retrieval
capability. An authorized command with an expected dossier revision must accept
or reject each proposal. A stale proposal remains an immutable rejected attempt
and cannot win through last-write-wins behavior.

## Stage-by-stage model contract

The stages below refine, but do not replace, the full protocol. `Human gate`
means a qualified role at the applicable harm tier. A stage may later replace a
mandatory human gate with a deterministic acceptance rule only after the
promotion protocol in this document passes prospectively for that exact domain,
language, source type, model, prompt, and intended use.

| Stage | Permitted model role | Forbidden model authority | Required output and gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Claim atomization** | Propose atomic subclaims, logical links, claim family candidates, ambiguity, presuppositions, and structured subject/predicate/object, polarity, quantifier, modality, population, geography, time, unit, threshold, comparator, definition, and causal force. | Invent missing scope; drop a condition, negation, comparator, modality, exception, or attribution; merge entities; silently strengthen wording; decide truth or material semantic equivalence. | `ClaimProposal` with original spans, round-trip reconstruction, lost-or-added-information warnings, alternatives, and abstention. Schema and semantic-diff checks run first; a human approves material claim identity. |
| **Query diversification** | Propose separate support, refutation, correction, retraction, primary-source, synonym, entity-alias, temporal, multilingual, archival, and alternative-hypothesis queries. | Declare coverage complete; choose a convenient stopping rule after seeing results; omit an opposition route; change claim scope; bypass source, rights, safety, or budget policy. | `QueryPlanProposal` with purpose, language, target source class, expected signal, exclusions, and relation to a frozen hypothesis. Deterministic policy checks and the research-plan authority approve the plan before synthesis. |
| **Retrieval and ranking** | Encode, search, rerank, cluster, deduplicate provisionally, and prioritize candidates for review. Expose lexical, semantic, source-specific, and recency signals separately. | Treat rank as evidence quality; hide low-ranked candidates from the ledger; admit an artifact; infer absence; decide observational independence; allow a provider-managed answer to substitute for captured sources. | `RankedCandidateProposal` names the complete received candidate set, provider/index snapshot, original ranks and scores, reranking version, truncation, and cluster hypotheses. Acquisition, rights, origin, and evidence gates remain external. |
| **Extraction and locators** | Propose exact text spans, page regions, table cells and headers, timestamps, transcript intervals, figure regions, structured values, units, and surrounding context. | Generate or repair a quotation from memory; normalize away a negation, uncertainty, denominator, unit, footnote, or table header; cite a derived translation as if it were the source; assert support. | `EvidenceLocatorProposal` resolves to an immutable artifact digest and parent transformation. Deterministic locator, byte, table, number, unit, and context checks run before independent review. Unresolvable text is not a quote. |
| **Translation** | Propose a purpose-specific translation, terminology alternatives, alignment between source and translated spans, and uncertainty about idiom or specialist meaning. | Declare two claim expressions semantically identical; erase hedging, modality, jurisdictional or scientific terminology; replace the source locator; translate or adapt a licensed method without permission. | `TranslationProposal` retains source text, aligned spans, named terminology, model/tool version, alternatives, and change warnings. Material legal, scientific, safety, or high-harm translations require competent bilingual review; back-translation alone is not acceptance. |
| **Entailment and scope** | Classify whether a located passage appears to support, refute, partially support, merely mention, or leave a claim insufficiently addressed; compare population, time, definitions, quantities, causal force, and other scope fields. | Decide that the passage or source is true; convert textual entailment into real-world support; ignore a material qualifier; force binary support/refute; treat lack of support as contradiction; issue a dossier verdict. | `EntailmentScopeProposal` contains separate relation and applicability fields, every mismatch, decisive spans, calibrated score only when valid, and `insufficient`/`out_of_distribution`. Deterministic numeric checks and an evidence reviewer accept or reject it. |
| **Origin and lineage** | Propose DOI, registry, author, publisher, institution, ownership, funding, dataset, witness, citation, syndication, transformation, mirror, and near-duplicate relations with supporting metadata. | Authenticate an actor from prose alone; declare independence from different URLs, vendors, models, or signatures; create a scalar source-trust score; collapse unresolved lineage into independent corroboration. | `OriginAssertionProposal` contains edge type, endpoints, supporting records, chronology, uncertainty, and competing origin hypotheses. Deterministic identity checks and a provenance reviewer create any canonical assertion; `unknown` remains first-class. |
| **Contradiction path** | Generate alternative hypotheses, predicted observations, counterexamples, correction/retraction searches, null/adverse-result searches, and challenges to scope, methods, origin, or inference. A blind-first model or reviewer may inspect the dossier without the proposed outcome. | Suppress a challenge because another model disagrees; rewrite the claim to defeat the challenge; stop after superficial opposition; declare an objection resolved; count model dissent as counterevidence. | `ContradictionProposal` records hypothesis, target premise, query amendments, candidate evidence, severity, and unresolved questions. The immutable counterevidence ledger and an adversarial reviewer decide disposition. No majority vote closes it. |
| **Synthesis** | Assemble a draft claim-evidence matrix, separate support and contradiction, surface dependence, missing evidence, alternative explanations, sensitivity, temporal limits, disagreements, and a proposed assurance vector beneath the profile ceiling. | Average assurance dimensions; vote by source or model count; turn citation support into truth; infer causality from association; hide dissent; set public wording; grant or transition assurance. | `SynthesisProposal` references only accepted dossier objects, distinguishes facts from generated interpretation, and carries residual unknowns and abstention. Qualified reviewers own specialist inference; the kernel alone checks issue predicates. |
| **Monitoring** | Compare known source or metadata versions, summarize diffs, classify possible correction/retraction/method/model events, propose affected dependencies, and prioritize review. | Fetch outside acquisition policy; declare a certificate fresh; silently regenerate an old stage with a new model; automatically flip, restore, or withdraw an epistemic result; discard an alert as harmless without trace. | `MonitoringTriggerProposal` names old/new immutable inputs, changed spans or fields, possible impact, affected IDs, and confidence envelope. Deterministic TTL, signature, feed, and dependency rules create safe states; humans assess materiality. Uncertainty defaults to `needs_review`, `stale`, or explicit monitoring gap according to policy. |

### Cross-stage constraints

1. A later stage cannot repair a missing earlier object by inventing it.
2. Every evidence-bearing field must resolve to a canonical reference or be
   explicitly marked generated, unknown, or unsupported.
3. A proposal accepted at one stage is not automatically admissible at the
   next stage.
4. The contradiction path receives its own budget and stopping rule; it is not
   a prompt suffix on supportive synthesis.
5. Translation, OCR, transcription, extraction, and summarization remain
   transformations with parent links. None becomes a source artifact by
   convenience.
6. Models may recommend abstention at any stage. They may not veto a mandatory
   deterministic or human abstention.

## Structured output contract

Provider-specific text never becomes canonical state. Each call first creates
an immutable `AIStageAttempt`; only a validated stage proposal can enter the
review workspace.

### Invocation manifest

The exact machine schema remains an implementation artifact. Its human contract
must contain at least:

```text
AIStageAttempt
  attempt_id
  stage_name
  stage_contract_version
  dossier_revision
  parent_attempt_ids[]
  authority = proposal_only
  purpose_and_intended_use
  input_refs[]
    canonical_id
    exact_revision_or_digest
    selected_range_or_field?
    transformation_parent?
    rights_and_visibility_class
  prompt_manifest
    template_id
    template_version
    template_digest
    resolved_prompt_digest
    retained_prompt_ref_or_replay_restriction
    role_message_order
    variable_bindings_digest
  model_manifest
    provider
    model_identifier
    immutable_revision_or_checkpoint_if_available
    endpoint_and_api_version
    provider_system_fingerprint_if_available
    deployment_region?
    tokenizer_runtime_quantization_hardware_if_self_hosted?
    safety_and_content_filter_versions
  inference_manifest
    temperature
    top_p
    max_output
    seed_if_supported
    response_format
    tool_choice_policy
  tool_calls[]
    tool_and_schema_version
    authorized_arguments
    raw_response_ref_and_digest
    index_or_corpus_snapshot?
  output_manifest
    raw_response_ref_and_digest
    finish_or_refusal_status
    stage_schema_id_and_version
    parser_and_validator_versions
    normalized_proposal_ref
    validation_failures[]
    repair_or_retry_parent?
  evaluation_context
    calibration_profile_and_expiry?
    in_or_out_of_distribution_result
    human_or_rule_acceptance
    reason_codes[]
  timing_usage_and_cost
  privacy_rights_and_replayability_status
```

For hosted models, the manifest records the most specific model revision the
provider exposes. An alias plus invocation date is still an opaque version and
must be marked as such. For self-hosted models it records checkpoint and
tokenizer digests, runtime, quantization, inference library, container, and
relevant hardware. A nominally identical seed or temperature zero does not
upgrade a nondeterministic service to deterministic replay.

Prompts are retained as exact access-controlled bytes when lawful and safe.
If privacy, source rights, security, or provider terms prohibit that retention,
the manifest records the restriction and the assurance profile lowers the
replay claim. A digest proves identity only when an authorized auditor can also
obtain the underlying bytes; it does not make inaccessible content replayable.
Secrets, credentials, unrestricted tools, and data outside the declared purpose
must never enter the prompt.

### Stage proposal envelope

Every stage-specific schema shares these semantics:

```text
StageProposal
  schema_version
  attempt_id
  input_refs[]
  proposals[]
    typed_value
    source_or_evidence_refs[]
    generated_fields[]
    reason_codes[]
    material_uncertainties[]
    alternatives[]
    scope_or_applicability?
    calibrated_score_and_envelope?
  abstain_reason?
  out_of_distribution_reasons[]
  warnings[]
```

Schemas use explicit enums and distinguish `unknown`, `not_reported`,
`not_checked`, `not_applicable`, and `unresolvable`. Missing fields do not
default to favorable values. Free text may explain a proposal, but cannot carry
an untyped authority transition.

Validation has four independent layers:

1. **Syntactic:** strict JSON Schema or equivalent validation, type and enum
   checks, bounded sizes, and no undeclared authority fields.
2. **Referential:** every ID, digest, locator, tool result, parent, and selected
   input resolves to the frozen attempt context.
3. **Semantic and domain:** deterministic invariants plus the required human
   checks for qualifier preservation, numbers, units, scope, meaning, and
   method applicability.
4. **Authority:** a policy-authorized command accepts the proposal against an
   expected dossier revision.

`schema_valid = true` proves only conformance to the declared schema.
[JSONSchemaBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10868) contains roughly 10,000
real-world schemas and finds material support differences among constrained-
decoding frameworks. Grammar-constrained decoding can improve structural
validity, but it cannot validate evidence semantics; naive constraints can also
distort generation quality
([Grammar-Aligned Decoding](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/2bdc2267c3d7d01523e2e17ac0a754f3-Abstract-Conference.html)).

An invalid response is retained. A bounded repair call creates a new attempt
linked to the failure; it does not overwrite it. The orchestrator may repair
syntax but may not retry until a desired epistemic label appears.

## No chain-of-thought dependency

The subsystem neither requests nor relies on hidden chain-of-thought as an
audit artifact. Provider-private reasoning may be unavailable, unstable,
policy-restricted, or unrelated to the decisive evidence. Even when a provider
returns a reasoning summary, it is generated text, not proof that the cited
reasoning caused or justifies the output.

Auditability instead comes from observable objects:

- exact retained inputs or explicit replay restrictions;
- resolved prompts and tool contracts where lawful;
- source and locator references;
- typed alternatives, uncertainty, and reason codes;
- raw and normalized outputs;
- deterministic validator and policy results;
- independent human decisions and preserved disagreement; and
- the final kernel predicate trace.

A reviewer must be able to reject a proposal from these objects without
reconstructing private model cognition. A short evidence-bound explanation can
help review, but it is itself checked against the evidence.

## Replay and reproducibility limits

The word `replay` names four different properties and must never collapse them.

| Replay class | Defensible claim | Hard limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Historical invocation replay** | The system can show the exact retained inputs, manifest, raw response, parsed proposal, validation, and disposition that occurred. | This proves what the service recorded, not that the proposal was correct or that the provider internally ran the declared model honestly. |
| **Deterministic control replay** | Schema checks, hashes, locator resolution, numerical rules, policy predicates, stage transitions, and kernel decisions reproduce on frozen artifacts and executable versions. | The rules may omit a real-world defect; restricted source bytes may prevent an external rerun. |
| **Self-hosted model rerun** | A pinned checkpoint/runtime/environment can be executed again and compared under a declared tolerance. | Hardware, kernels, concurrency, floating-point behavior, sampling, and hidden nondeterminism may prevent byte-identical output. Semantic equivalence still needs an evaluation rule. |
| **Hosted model re-invocation** | The system can submit a captured request again and record the new result. | It normally cannot prove use of identical weights, routing, safety layers, retrieval state, or hardware. A re-invocation is a new attempt, not reproduction of the old one. |

The original response remains frozen in the historical dossier. It is never
silently replaced by output from a newer model. A model, prompt, parser, tool,
retriever, index, or safety-policy change creates a new component version,
invalidates the applicable calibration envelope, and triggers the comparison or
requalification specified by policy.

Longitudinal studies have observed material behavior changes behind hosted
model services, although some apparent changes were caused by output-format and
instruction-following differences rather than pure capability decay
([Chen et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009)). Reproducibility work
also finds stochastic variation under settings commonly treated as
deterministic
([Towards Reproducible LLM Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03492)).
Those studies justify continuous measurement and version capture; they do not
justify assuming every update is worse.

## Calibration, selective prediction, and abstention

### No universal confidence

Model log probabilities, verbal confidence, consistency across samples, and
agreement across models are diagnostic features, not probabilities that a
claim is true. Calibration is admitted only for one named stage, label
definition, harm profile, domain, language, source type, time period, and
evaluation population.

Each calibrated stage reports:

- risk-coverage and area-under-risk-coverage curves;
- false-accept, false-support, false-refute, and missed-counterevidence rates;
- one-sided confidence bounds on harm-critical rates;
- Brier score, expected calibration error, and reliability plots where a
  probability is meaningful;
- abstention and over-refusal rates;
- slices by claim family, language, time, source, format, ambiguity, and harm;
- reviewer effort, latency, and cost over all attempts, including rejected and
  abstained cases; and
- calibration version, evaluation window, expiry, and drift triggers.

Thresholds are pre-registered per stage and harm tier. There is no subsystem-
wide `95% confident` threshold and no arithmetic truth score. A stage outside
its envelope returns `out_of_distribution` and falls back; it does not borrow
calibration from a nearby benchmark.

### Stage blocks map to canonical abstention

An AI stage returns a typed `stage_block_reason`; it does not mint a competing
public abstention vocabulary or decide the terminal run disposition. The
workflow and assurance kernel map one or more stage blocks to the closed
`AbstentionAttestation.primary_reason` enum owned by the
[assurance model](assurance-model.md#abstention-policy-and-attestation):

| AI `stage_block_reason` | Canonical primary reason candidate |
| --- | --- |
| `scope_unresolved` | `scope_unresolved` |
| `insufficient_or_conflicting_evidence` | `insufficient_evidence` or `material_counterevidence_unresolved`, selected by a deterministic predicate |
| `source_or_locator_unavailable` | `evidence_inaccessible` |
| `translation_or_transformation_unreliable` | `insufficient_evidence` or `replay_failed`, according to the failed required predicate |
| `origin_or_independence_unresolved` | `dependence_unresolved` |
| `uncalibrated` or `out_of_distribution` | `outside_method_envelope` |
| `qualified_review_required` | `qualified_review_unavailable` |
| `security_or_poisoning_signal` | `rights_or_safety_block` while quarantined |
| `rights_or_privacy_blocked` | `rights_or_safety_block` |
| `provider_or_monitoring_unavailable` | `evidence_inaccessible`, `temporal_requirements_unmet`, or technical `failed`, selected by explicit workflow state |

The mapping is versioned, fail-closed, and retained with the run. A model may
propose the block; it cannot choose a more convenient canonical reason. Several
stage blocks can coexist, while the attestation records one primary and any
number of secondary reasons. `Stale` is never an abstention reason: it is a
certificate lifecycle value.

Raw accuracy is an unsafe optimization target when abstention is scored as an
ordinary error. A 2026 study over SimpleQA found that consistency-based
mitigation reduced both errors and raw accuracy; under higher penalties all
four unmitigated systems scored below always abstaining
([Nature, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10549-w)).
[FactTest](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/nie25a.html) supplies a useful
finite-sample selective-testing framework and reports large gains from
abstention on studied QA tasks, but its guarantee depends on the calibration
setup and does not survive arbitrary deployment drift.

Semantic entropy is a useful auxiliary disagreement signal: the original study
reported mean AUROC 0.790 across 30 task/model combinations, versus 0.691 for
naive entropy
([Nature, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)). It can
still miss a stable, confidently repeated false belief and requires several
samples plus semantic clustering. It therefore helps decide when to review; it
does not authorize acceptance.

## Model and agent dependence

Multiple calls are not multiple evidence origins. The following remain one
correlated computational family unless evidence demonstrates otherwise:

- several agents using the same base model;
- different prompts, roles, temperatures, or random seeds;
- a generator and judge from the same provider or model family;
- different vendors trained on overlapping public corpora, benchmark answers,
  synthetic data, or distilled outputs;
- several models reading the same poisoned retrieval set; and
- several applications backed by the same hidden provider or search API.

An ICML 2025 analysis of more than 350 models found that, on one studied
leaderboard, two wrong models selected the same wrong answer about 60 percent of
the time
([Correlated Errors in Large Language Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html)).
The rate is not portable to this engine, but it falsifies the independence
assumption behind naive majority voting. Evaluator models can also favor their
own generations
([Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Abstract-Conference.html)).

Panels of different model families may reduce an individual judge's bias and
cost on some evaluation tasks
([Replacing Judges with Juries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)). That is a
useful counterpoint, but improved task performance is not proof of epistemic
independence. The subsystem may use diverse models to generate alternatives,
measure disagreement, or prioritize human review. It must not:

- count model votes as sources;
- convert consensus into origin independence or assurance;
- let one family generate the gold labels that promote that family;
- resolve material disagreement by majority; or
- call vendors independent without tracing shared model, data, retriever,
  provider, and evaluation dependencies.

For high-harm stages, a proposal and its only semantic checker must not use the
same model family. Even cross-family agreement remains a gate signal, not an
acceptance predicate.

### Configuration choice, not agent-count theater

The initial architecture does not choose “many agents” as a generic quality
strategy. Each configuration must beat the simpler one on prospective
stage-specific harm, coverage, reviewer effort, latency, and cost:

| Configuration | Legitimate use | Material failure | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One model pass | Cheap candidate generation on a bounded stage. | One prompt/model/retrieval failure becomes the only proposal. | Baseline only; never sufficient for a material semantic gate. |
| Several roles/prompts on one model family | Generate query, interpretation, or objection diversity. | Correlated knowledge and failure remain; role names create false independence. | Use only when measured incremental recall exceeds cost; count as one computational lineage. |
| Several model/provider families | Surface common-mode differences or provide provider fallback. | Training data, retriever, benchmark, infrastructure, or judge dependence may remain hidden. | Optional diagnostic/availability layer; never source independence or voting authority. |
| Deterministic checker plus one or more models | Validate schema, digest, locator, calculation, policy predicate, and canonical output around model proposals. | The checker may validate the wrong specification and cannot judge the world. | Required wherever the property is fully specifiable; preferred over another model vote. |
| Qualified human review plus deterministic controls and bounded model assistance | Resolve scoped semantic/method judgments while preserving machine invariants. | Human error, anchoring, capture, cost, and scarce competence remain. | Default for any issued result until the exact human gate is prospectively proven replaceable. |

The promotion experiment begins with one-pass and no-model baselines, then adds
one mechanism at a time. A multi-role or multi-provider configuration is
rejected when it produces no preregistered material benefit, when its errors
remain common-mode, or when equivalent value comes from deterministic checks or
targeted human review at lower total harm and lifecycle cost.

## Distribution shift and benchmark contamination

Public static benchmarks are development evidence, not launch evidence. Their
questions, translations, labels, rationales, or derivative examples may appear
in training or fine-tuning data. Exact-string decontamination is insufficient:
cross-language translations can evade lexical overlap while retaining answer
knowledge
([Cross-lingual contamination](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.990/)).
A controlled detector study reported 92--100 percent accuracy on seven datasets
and found contamination evidence for a GPT-4 test split, but detector accuracy
inside that experiment is not proof that an arbitrary model or benchmark is
clean
([Time Travel in LLMs](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/bc39a59c49b731c51398ad6b12f301d3-Abstract-Conference.html)).

Every stage promotion therefore uses:

1. a public benchmark only for comparability and failure discovery;
2. a private, human-adjudicated development set;
3. a hidden temporal holdout created after the evaluated model or provider
   version was frozen where feasible;
4. semantic, paraphrase, translation, citation, and metadata decontamination;
5. fresh adversarial, poisoned, ambiguous, and abstention-correct cases;
6. slices unavailable to prompt and model developers before threshold freeze;
7. a prospective shadow period; and
8. a new holdout after any tuning on observed failures.

[LiveBench](https://livebench.ai/) reduces exposure by adding recent,
objectively scored questions. It should be described as contamination-reduced,
not contamination-free. Domain, temporal, format, and policy shift can still
invalidate a clean score. Any change to model weights or alias, prompt, tool,
retriever, index, source mix, language mix, claim mix, or output schema triggers
an OOD and calibration review.

## Provider portability and exit

The subsystem interface is stage-semantic, not provider-semantic. Canonical
objects contain no provider-specific completion type, citation object, search
answer, safety label, embedding, or reasoning field. Adapters normalize those
objects into the versioned proposal and attempt contracts while retaining the
raw provider response for audit.

Portability requires:

- provider-neutral stage schemas and explicit capability manifests;
- prompt templates separated from provider transport and tool syntax;
- a conservative common subset for required structured output, with external
  validation even when native schema mode is available;
- captured provider-managed search queries, result identifiers, ranks,
  snippets, citations, and snapshot limitations rather than trusting an answer
  object;
- normalized refusal, truncation, timeout, rate-limit, safety, and usage states;
- independent data-residency, retention, training-use, confidentiality, and
  rights approval per provider;
- a deterministic or manual baseline for every material stage;
- at least one tested exit route for each promoted stage; and
- dual-run and prospective recalibration before a replacement accepts live
  authority-bearing work.

Portability does not mean models are interchangeable. A provider replacement is
a new component version and cannot inherit the old model's calibration,
promotion, or privacy approval. An outage falls through this safe ladder:

1. deterministic baseline if it satisfies the same stage contract;
2. a previously qualified alternative inside its own valid envelope;
3. manual processing by the required role; or
4. typed abstention, `stale`, or monitoring gap.

The orchestrator never routes protected content to an unapproved vendor merely
to preserve availability. A provider that cannot expose stable model identity,
raw tool results, required retention controls, or enough invocation metadata
may support private exploratory work but cannot support a replay-claimed tier.

## Injection and retrieval-poisoning boundary

All retrieved text, metadata, images, OCR, transcripts, tool responses, and
model-produced summaries are untrusted data. The subsystem assumes that some
will contain instructions specifically designed to alter its behavior.

Mandatory controls are:

- source bytes and control instructions remain separately identified; quoted
  source content never gains a system or tool role;
- models receive no secrets and only the minimum stage-specific data;
- retrieval and tool execution occur through deterministic brokers with
  allowlisted schemas, arguments, destinations, byte limits, and rights checks;
- a model can propose a tool request but cannot authorize it;
- active content, hidden text, Unicode anomalies, rendered/extracted
  divergence, and instruction-like passages are preserved and flagged;
- extraction must resolve every material value to an exact locator;
- search ledgers retain received candidates and provider ranks before model
  reranking;
- primary-source routes, support and refutation routes, origin clustering, and
  per-lineage caps reduce rank manipulation;
- poison detection is advisory; a detector's silence never establishes safety;
- suspicious inputs quarantine dependent attempts and cannot be retried through
  a more permissive model; and
- an incident query can enumerate every attempt and dossier by artifact,
  passage, model, prompt, retriever, index, provider, and time window.

[AgentDojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352) evaluates 97 realistic tasks and
629 security test cases and finds both task failures and incomplete protection
from existing prompt-injection defenses. It is an agent/tool benchmark, not a
proof of this design. [PoisonedRAG](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zou-poisonedrag)
demonstrates targeted retrieval poisoning; its attacks do not estimate the
prevalence of real campaigns, but they invalidate the assumption that retrieved
relevance implies benign evidence.

The promotion gate is not “the model ignored all malicious instructions.” It
is that no tested untrusted input can authorize an action, access a secret,
escape its tenant, erase a candidate, alter policy, write canonical state, or
produce a strong result without the independent evidence and gates that would
have been required had the poisoned item not existed. Adaptive attacks remain
part of every release evaluation.

## Evaluation programme

### Experimental unit and references

The unit is one stage attempt on frozen inputs, not a polished end-to-end
answer. Reference labels are created by the qualified role for that stage,
preserve pre-adjudication disagreement, and separate ambiguous from incorrect.
Evaluators are blinded to provider and model where feasible. The same model
cannot both create the reference and receive promotion credit from it.

Every evaluation compares at least:

- a no-model deterministic or rules baseline;
- the current manual workflow and its time, disagreement, and error;
- the previous promoted model, if one exists; and
- the candidate model under identical frozen inputs and budgets.

The goal is not to beat humans on one average score. It is to reduce total work
or improve recall while keeping the harm-critical upper confidence bound,
abstention behavior, subgroup performance, security boundary, and downstream
stage quality inside the pre-registered budget.

### Stage evaluations and baselines

| Stage | Minimum baseline and evaluation set | Primary measures; unsafe shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim atomization | Human claim editor, a deterministic sentence/clause splitter, Claimify/VeriFact cases, and a private set with nested conditions, numbers, modality, causality, attribution, and multilingual context. | Material qualifier omission/addition, entailment to original, element coverage, over-decomposition, ambiguity detection, downstream verdict changes, and review minutes. Claim count or atomicity alone is unsafe. |

Query novelty or count alone is unsafe. |
| Retrieval and ranking | BM25 or native database search, official-source connectors, human screening, FEVER, SciFact, AVeriTeC, CRAG, and a hidden temporal/poisoned corpus. | Complete evidence-set recall@k, counterevidence recall, origin diversity, poison robustness, inaccessible-source visibility, reviewer work, and latency. nDCG, answer accuracy, or citation presence alone is unsafe. |
| Extraction and locators | Deterministic parsers and exact search plus human extraction over HTML, PDFs, scans, tables, spreadsheets, audio/video, versions, and adversarial layouts. | Locator resolution, exact-span/cell/time accuracy, number/unit/denominator/negation preservation, context sufficiency, invented-quote rate, and parent-transformation trace. | Translation | Qualified bilingual reference plus terminology rules over ordinary, legal, scientific, idiomatic, negated, numerical, and low-resource cases. | Claim-equivalence error, qualifier/entity/number/unit preservation, source-span alignment, terminology consistency, uncertainty detection, and reviewer time. ### Primary benchmark evidence and its limits

No benchmark validates the complete chain. The following results are used to
design tests, not to claim deployment readiness.

| Source | Bounded result used | Why it is insufficient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Claimify](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.348/) | On BingCheck, the authors report 99.0 percent entailment, 91.2 macro-F1 sentence coverage, 83.7 element coverage, and 80.5 percent satisfactory decontextualization. | One main corpus and substantial model-based evaluation; high entailment does not guarantee complete qualifier preservation or safe downstream use. |
| [VeriFact / FactRBench](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.905/) | Human correction of extraction changed the downstream verification verdict in 24.9 percent of audited cases. | References and judges can themselves be incomplete; it establishes material extraction risk, not a universal rate. |
| [FEVER](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1074/) | 185,445 synthetic Wikipedia claims; the original baseline scored 31.87 when correct evidence was required versus 50.91 label accuracy without that requirement. | Artificial claims, a frozen Wikipedia corpus, and no claim extraction or open-Web coverage. |
| [SciFact](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.609/) | 1,409 expert-written claims and 5,183 abstracts; open selection-plus-label reached 39.5 F1 versus 60.6 with oracle abstracts. | Abstracts only, supplied claims, and no multi-study scientific synthesis; one paper's entailment is not scientific truth. |
| [AVeriTeC](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/cd86a30526cd1aff61d6f89f107634e4-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html) and [2024 shared task](https://aclanthology.org/2024.fever-1.1/) | 4,568 real claims from 50 fact-checking organizations; the best 2024 system reached 0.63 on the task score. | Web evidence is incomplete and mutable; alternative valid evidence is hard to score, and automatic evidence similarity correlated poorly with human adequacy in the task analysis. |
| [AVeriTeC 2.0](https://fever.ai/2025/task.html) | The official 2025 task reports a top Ev2R score of 0.3317 plus or minus 0.0015 versus a 0.2023 plus or minus 0.0068 baseline under a fixed execution environment. | Its revised metric and test set are not directly comparable to 2024; an LLM participates in evidence evaluation, so the score remains judge-dependent. |
| [CRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04744) | The authors report at most 34 percent accuracy for advanced LLMs, 44 percent for naive RAG, and only 63 percent hallucination-free answers for studied industry systems despite retrievable gold facts for most questions. | 2024 systems and task definitions; search availability does not establish source admissibility, origin independence, or claim truth. |
| [RAGTruth](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585/) | Prompted GPT-4-turbo reached 63.4 response-level F1 for hallucination detection; a specialized fine-tuned Llama-2-13B reached 78.7. | Dataset annotation and generation families are bounded; a general frontier judge is not automatically a reliable verifier. |
| [ALCE](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/) | The best studied ELI5 systems lacked complete citation support in roughly half of responses; the automatic irrelevant-citation detector had 66.1 percent precision. | Citation completeness is not source truth or corpus completeness, and NLI struggles with partial support. |
| [DeepFactBench](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1586/) | DeepFact-Eval with GPT-5 reached 87.2 percent accuracy and 89.9 F1 on the reported test; the pipeline cost about USD 1.16 and 516,900 input tokens per claim. | Twenty reports and six expert domains; expensive iterative auditing, evolving gold labels, and no proof of safe autonomous issue. |
| [Semantic entropy](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0) | Mean AUROC 0.790 across the reported task/model combinations. | Detects inconsistent semantic answers more readily than stable shared misconceptions; costly multi-sampling and an entailment model remain in the loop. |
| [Correlated model errors](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html) | More than 350 models were analyzed; same-error concentration remained high, including about 60 percent on one leaderboard when both models were wrong. | Task and model populations differ from this engine; it is a warning against independence assumptions, not a calibrated correlation for future providers. |
| [JSONSchemaBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10868) | Roughly 10,000 real-world schemas expose large framework coverage and performance differences. | Structural conformance says nothing about semantic fidelity, source support, authorization, or truth. |

SimpleQA is deliberately narrow: 4,326 stable, single-answer questions and no
retrieval or long-form evidence synthesis
([official paper](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/simpleqa.pdf)). Static benchmark
success must therefore never be extrapolated to temporal, scientific, legal,
causal, multilingual, or open-world dossiers.

## Promotion, fallback, and rollback

### Promotion ladder

Each stage moves independently through:

1. **offline diagnostic** — no dossier use;
2. **shadow** — runs beside the manual reference and cannot affect ordering or
   decisions;
3. **reviewer proposal** — visible only to an authorized reviewer, with every
   acceptance, correction, rejection, and time cost recorded;
4. **bounded operational assistance** — may prioritize or prefill low-risk work
   while a deterministic or human gate still accepts it; and
5. **narrow deterministic acceptance** — available only for a fully specified
   T0 subtask whose machine-checkable output and prospective evaluation satisfy
   the frozen method profile. The model still cannot issue a certificate.

Promotion requires all of the following:

- a frozen stage contract, intended use, harm budget, thresholds, and rollback
  rule before the hidden set is opened;
- superiority or justified non-inferiority to the simplest baseline on the
  harm-critical measures, not just average accuracy;
- acceptable one-sided bounds for false acceptance and missed decisive
  counterevidence, plus sufficient sample size for material slices;
- calibrated selective behavior and correct OOD abstention on prospective
  temporal cases;
- no critical injection, poisoning, cross-tenant, rights, or tool-authorization
  failure;
- preserved performance by language, source type, format, time, claim family,
  and harm slice, with disparity investigated rather than averaged away;
- measured net reviewer work and cost including rejected proposals, appeals,
  and monitoring;
- independent reproduction of the evaluation and audit of a sample of gold
  labels; and
- a successful shadow period and rollback drill.

A stage promotion does not promote the next stage or the end-to-end system.
Public benchmark improvement, vendor assurance, model size, several-agent
agreement, a schema-valid response, or a successful retrospective replay is
insufficient on its own.

### Safe fallback

Every promoted stage has a pretested fallback with equivalent input and output
semantics. Fallback never means silently changing provider, broadening a prompt,
dropping a validator, using an uncalibrated judge, or lowering an abstention
threshold. When no qualified path is available, the safe result is partial
research output or a manual queue while the run remains open;
`out_of_distribution` is a stage block that can lead to canonical
`outside_method_envelope`; and a terminal run may emit an
`AbstentionAttestation`. Separately, an already-issued certificate whose
freshness gate expires transitions to lifecycle `stale`. None of these states
may be flattened into one model-selected label.

The system automatically suspends or rolls back a stage when:

- a model, alias, prompt, schema, parser, tool, retriever, index, source policy,
  or provider control changes outside the qualified version;
- prospective error, calibration, abstention, subgroup, security, privacy,
  rights, cost, or latency crosses its frozen limit;
- a benchmark contamination or gold-label defect invalidates promotion
  evidence;
- an appeal, incident, or audit identifies a common-mode failure; or
- the required manifest, original output, or deterministic validation cannot be
  recovered.

Rollback identifies every affected attempt and dossier. It creates review or
restriction work; it never silently recomputes historical outputs with a newer
model.

## Falsifiers and stop conditions

The AI stage must be removed, narrowed, or kept permanently advisory if any of
the following persists after one predeclared remediation cycle:

- human correction continues to change a material claim, locator, scope,
  origin, contradiction, or synthesis result above the stage's harm budget;
- verifying model proposals consumes as much or more qualified work than the
  accepted work it saves;
- the simple deterministic or manual baseline matches safety and throughput at
  lower cost or greater portability;
- decisive counterevidence, null/adverse evidence, corrections, retractions, or
  low-resource-language sources are repeatedly missed;
- false support, false independence, invented quotation, scope strengthening,
  or translation drift exceeds the frozen upper bound;
- risk-coverage calibration does not transfer to the prospective set and
  abstention cannot restore the declared risk;
- vendor or agent consensus remains wrong on common-mode cases and reviewers
  cannot detect the pattern sustainably;
- an untrusted artifact can change tool behavior, hide a candidate, leak data,
  write state, or obtain a stronger result through poisoning;
- a provider swap materially changes accepted dossier content despite the same
  stage contract and no sustainable recalibration path exists;
- private temporal results fail to reproduce public benchmark gains;
- model or provider updates occur faster than the team can requalify them;
- rights, privacy, confidentiality, or source restrictions prevent the capture
  needed for the promised audit and no lower-claim alternative is useful;
- monitoring misses injected material events or creates an unaffordable false-
  alert and review burden; or
- users or reviewers continue to treat AI proposals as proof after two
  workflow and presentation revisions.

The subsystem as a whole should be rejected if stage-wise assistance does not
improve the first vertical slice's safely completed work, counterevidence
coverage, correction latency, or reviewer burden enough to justify its model
risk and operating cost. The fallback product is a narrower deterministic and
human evidence-dossier workflow, not a weaker truth claim.

## Residual unknowns

This research does not yet establish:

- which provider or open model is best for any stage;
- a defensible numerical threshold for a real harm profile;
- representative performance in low-resource languages, inaccessible sources,
  multimodal artifacts, or adversarial scientific and legal material;
- a reliable automatic measure of observational independence;
- stable provider identifiers or replay guarantees across commercial APIs;
- whether qualified reviewers save net time after checking model proposals;
- how often real retrieval poisoning or indirect injection will appear;
- whether users can understand the distinction between an AI proposal, a
  reviewed evidence use, and an issued certificate; or
- whether any stage beyond bounded T0 transformations can safely receive
  deterministic acceptance authority.

These are launch-blocking evaluation questions for the affected tier, not
details to infer from model capability or benchmark rank.
