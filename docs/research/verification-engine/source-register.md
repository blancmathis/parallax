# Verification-engine source register

## Summary

This register is a bounded material-source inventory for the active
verification-engine research proposal. It identifies the external sources that
materially support the dossier, the additional checked sources needed to close
important product and evaluation gaps, and the relationships that prevent
correlated material from being counted as independent corroboration.

Point-of-use linkage is generated separately in the
[source usage manifest](source-usage-manifest.md), which maps every external
citation occurrence back to the stable IDs owned here. That manifest proves a
join, not that the cited proposition is correct.

The register is deliberately conservative. A source establishes only the
bounded claim named here. A standard establishes its own contract, owner
documentation establishes intended behavior, an original study establishes a
result inside its sample and method, a synthesis inherits the limits of its
included studies, a preprint remains unreviewed, and an anecdote supplies only
a failure scenario. Inclusion never means endorsement or validation of the
proposed engine.

## Defines

Stable source IDs, canonical direct URLs, publication and consultation dates,
evidence type, provenance, conservative independence clusters, supported claim
families, material limits, access gaps, and expert-review requirements for this
research proposal, plus the explicit lossless bridge from local theoretical and
method-source extensions to the evidence-base owner vocabulary.

## Does not define

Accepted Parallax behavior, an approved architecture, a systematic review of
all relevant disciplines, legal advice, source truth, licence permission,
statistical independence, or proof that the proposed engine will work.

## Research status and register rules

- **Status:** active research proposal; not accepted Parallax truth.
- **Cutoff and consultation date:** 2026-08-12.
- **Canonicalization:** fragments, trailing-slash variants, PubMed/PMC mirrors,
  publisher pages, and DOI resolvers for the same work are represented once.
  A row can contain several URLs only when they are distinct parts or versions
  of one owner-maintained source family.
- **Checked source:** the source record or document was inspected directly for
  the bounded use stated here. It does not mean every assertion, citation,
  licence, later version, or downstream interpretation was independently
  verified.
- **Owner-reviewed import:** a row marked `owner-reviewed` was reconciled from a
  dependent research document whose material claim and limit are explicit, but
  the external source was not reopened during this register pass. It inventories
  the dependency; it is not a new independent verification.
- **Coverage:** reconciled against material citations in the dependent research
  documents at the cutoff. It is not a line-by-line bibliography: simple
  aliases, direct-download mirrors, decorative links, and non-material secondary
  references may be omitted or grouped with their source family.
- **Claim linkage at this research stage:** dependent prose currently cites
  canonical URLs rather than embedding `SRC-*`/`C-*` identifiers line by line.
  URL-to-row reconciliation is therefore the current audit bridge, not a claim
  that machine traceability already exists. Before design freeze, every
  requirement, decision, evaluation gate, and material quantitative statement
  receives a machine-readable link to one or more `SRC-*` rows, a bounded
  `C-*` family, exact location, supported proposition, and limitation; missing
  links fail the documentation gate rather than being inferred from proximity.
- **Cluster:** a conservative dependency warning, not a statistical finding.
  Sources in one cluster may share authors, institution, platform, dataset,
  standards process, tool ownership, or selection mechanism. Different cluster
  names do not prove independence.
- **Evidence type ownership:** the
  [evidence base](evidence-base.md#source-quality-vocabulary) owns the canonical
  cross-dossier vocabulary. This register uses two explicit local extensions,
  `ORIGINAL-THEORETICAL` and `ORIGINAL-METHOD`, because coercing either into
  `ORIGINAL-EMPIRICAL` or owner documentation would erase a material
  distinction. The mapping below is authoritative for this register until the
  evidence-base owner adopts or rejects those codes.
- **Anecdotes:** no `PUBLIC-FEEDBACK` anecdote currently supports a material
  architecture claim. Public forum reports observed during discovery remain
  excluded from the evidence tables until their exact content and provenance
  are archived; even then they can define test scenarios only.

### Cross-document evidence-type mapping

| Register code | Meaning in this register | Evidence-base owner code | Aggregation rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ORIGINAL-THEORETICAL` | Original theorem, formal model, impossibility result, or analytical proof whose conclusion is conditional on stated assumptions. | `null` — no lossless code exists in the owner vocabulary at the cutoff. | Preserve the local code and assumptions. Never aggregate it as empirical validation, official guidance, or deployment evidence. |
| `ORIGINAL-METHOD` | Original method or protocol proposal, whether or not its paper also reports an evaluation. | `null` — no lossless code exists in the owner vocabulary at the cutoff. | Preserve the local code and record empirical demonstrations separately in the limitations. Never count the proposal and its evaluation as two independent sources. |

A machine export therefore carries both `evidence_base_code` and
`register_extension_code`. Exactly one is non-null. Unknown codes fail schema
validation; consumers may not silently map either extension to
`ORIGINAL-EMPIRICAL`. If the evidence-base owner later adds a lossless category,
this table receives a versioned mapping and existing rows keep their historical
classification.

## Claim-family key

| Code | Bounded claim family |
| --- | --- |
| `C-LIMIT` | No mechanism in scope certifies unqualified, universal real-world truth; guarantees must name the object and trust boundary. |
| `C-PROV` | Provenance, signatures, hashes, registration, and log receipts establish narrow origin or integrity properties, not factual truth. |
| `C-OPEN` | Public information is open-world by default; failure to find evidence is not proof of absence. |
| `C-SCOPE` | Claim wording, population, time, definitions, version, and intended use materially constrain any result. |
| `C-SEARCH` | Support and counterevidence search must be reproducible, bounded, and explicit about inaccessible space and stopping rules. |
| `C-INDEP` | Independence is evaluated at observation, dataset, method, funding, and control lineage rather than URL or model count. |
| `C-HUMAN-AI` | AI can assist retrieval and analysis but cannot self-certify; human review is also fallible and needs competence and independence controls. |
| `C-LABEL` | Labels and explanations change belief and trust; incorrect or over-broad labels create false-authority harm. |
| `C-COMMUNITY` | Community consensus can improve usefulness, legitimacy, or distribution but is neither truth nor complete coverage. |
| `C-CORRECT` | Corrections, concerns, retractions, appeals, expiry, and supersession need typed events and propagation without automatic verdict inversion. |
| `C-SCIENCE` | Scientific assurance is outcome- and method-specific, uncertainty-bearing, specialist, and incapable of universal causal proof. |
| `C-REPRO` | Registration, reproducibility, robustness, replication, and generalization are distinct properties. |
| `C-FRESH` | Current or living evidence requires separate search, integration, publication, expiry, suspension, and retirement states. |
| `C-SEC` | Ingestion, retrieval, parsers, models, reviewers, operators, and upstream services are separate attack surfaces that must fail closed. |
| `C-CRYPTO` | Transparency and timestamp systems can make statements accountable and detectable, but do not validate their semantic truth. |
| `C-PRIVACY` | Purpose, minimization, lawful basis, access, retention, erasure, restriction, and propagation must be designed per data flow. |
| `C-RIGHTS` | Lawful access, text-and-data mining, retention, quotation, model processing, and redistribution are separate permissions. |
| `C-PERSON` | Publication about identifiable people creates independent defamation, privacy, presumption-of-innocence, reply, and safety duties. |
| `C-PLATFORM` | DSA and AI Act duties depend on the actual surface, intended purpose, and provider/deployer role. |
| `C-ARCH` | Product documentation can establish component capabilities and constraints; the hybrid architecture recommendation remains an unvalidated design judgment. |
| `C-EVAL` | Evaluation must be stage-specific, temporal, adversarial, independently adjudicated, and include abstention, coverage, cost, and harm. |
| `C-OPS` | Operational feasibility depends on measured workload, queues, staffing, service levels, correction and appeal load, resilience, and full lifecycle cost rather than model-call cost alone. |

## Evidence deliberately not established

The register does not establish a representative global error rate for
fact-checking, a stable rate of misinformation-label harm, a complete Web
search, a reliable automatic measure of source independence, market demand,
willingness to pay, reviewer supply, lawful evidence retention, a permanent
gold set, or the superiority of the proposed architecture over a narrower
citation-audit service.

No number of rows in this register changes those unknowns. Sources sharing a
cluster are never counted as independent replications, and sources from
different clusters are treated as potentially dependent until their underlying
data, authorship, funding, platform, and method lineage are checked for the
specific claim.

## Maintenance rule

When a source changes, is corrected, becomes inaccessible, is superseded, or
is found to share a hidden origin, update its row and review every dependent
research claim. Preserve the stable ID. Add a new ID only for a materially
different work or version whose claims and limits must be evaluated separately.
Do not silently replace a dated empirical result with a newer publication or a
standard with an incompatible version.
